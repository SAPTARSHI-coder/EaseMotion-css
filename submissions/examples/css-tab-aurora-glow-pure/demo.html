Atmospheric Interaction

Auroras are the result of disturbances in the magnetosphere caused by solar wind. These disturbances are sometimes strong enough to alter the trajectories of charged particles in both solar wind and magnetospheric plasma.

Altitude: 100km - 400km

Magnetic Deflection

The Earth's magnetic field directs these charged particles towards the polar regions. As they precipitate into the upper atmosphere, they collide with atoms and molecules, primarily oxygen and nitrogen.

Poles: North (Borealis) & South (Australis)

Solar Wind Dynamics

The energy emitted during these atmospheric collisions manifests as light. The distinct colors of the aurora are determined by the specific atmospheric gas involved in the collision and the altitude at which it occurs.

Speed: 400 - 800 km/s

Visual Phenomena

Pale green and pink are the most common colors, produced by oxygen molecules located about 60 miles above earth. Rare red auroras are produced by high-altitude oxygen, at heights of up to 200 miles.
