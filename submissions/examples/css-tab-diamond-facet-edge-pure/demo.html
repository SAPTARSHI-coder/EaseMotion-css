Crystalline Overview

A diamond is a solid form of the element carbon with its atoms arranged in a crystal structure called diamond cubic. At room temperature and pressure, another solid form of carbon known as graphite is the chemically stable form.

Lattice Structure

The most common crystal structure of diamond is called diamond cubic. It is formed of intersecting face-centered cubic lattices. Its highly symmetrical arrangement of atoms contributes to its extreme hardness.

Optical Properties

Diamonds have a very high refractive index (2.417) and moderate dispersion (0.044). These properties cause diamonds to exhibit high brilliance and a prismatic "fire" when properly cut.
